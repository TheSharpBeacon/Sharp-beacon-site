2026 NFL Power Rankings: The Bottom Five
By Ray · The Sharp Beacon
#32 Arizona Cardinals

Arizona finished 3-14, the worst record in the entire league, and it wasn't close — they went 2-0 to start the year, then lost 14 of their next 15. Kyler Murray tore his season apart with a mid-season foot injury, and backup Jacoby Brissett actually outplayed him statistically but the wins didn't come. Jonathan Gannon got fired after three straight losing seasons, and Mike LaFleur — Sean McVay's former OC — took over. The offseason story is Trey McBride, who set the NFL record for catches by a tight end, plus the draft pick of running back Jeremiyah Love at No. 3 overall. But heading into 2026, Arizona still hasn't settled the quarterback job — Jacoby Brissett's even in a contract standoff — and they play in the NFC West, arguably the toughest division in football, against the Rams, Seahawks and 49ers. Win total's sitting around 3.5 to 4.5. This is the shakiest outlook of any team in the league right now.

#31 Las Vegas Raiders

Also 3-14, and this was a historically bad offense — dead last in scoring, dead last in rushing efficiency for the second straight year. Pete Carroll lasted just one season before getting fired. The one bright spot was Maxx Crosby, who set a franchise tackle-for-loss record, and rookie running back Ashton Jeanty, who put up nearly 1,000 rushing yards despite a terrible offensive line. The reset for 2026 is aggressive — Klint Kubiak, fresh off winning a Super Bowl as Seattle's offensive coordinator, is the new head coach, and they used the number one overall pick on quarterback Fernando Mendoza, with Kirk Cousins signed as a bridge starter. There's real optimism here because the plan actually makes sense — but it's a rookie quarterback in the brutal AFC West, against Kansas City, the Chargers, and Denver. That's a lot to ask in year one.

#30 New York Jets

New York was historically bad in a very specific way — 3-14, and they became the first team since 1933 to go an entire season without recording a single interception. They were dead last in point differential, turnover differential, and yard differential, all at once — nobody's ever done that. Aaron Glenn's first year as head coach was rough, and they fired the defensive coordinator mid-season. The offseason was a total teardown and rebuild — they traded away both Sauce Gardner and Quinnen Williams at the deadline, then this offseason brought back Geno Smith at quarterback and traded away Justin Fields, while spending big in free agency on defense with Minkah Fitzpatrick and Demario Davis. The win total sits around 5.5, with some optimism building. But they're in the AFC East with Buffalo and New England, which is about as tough a path as there is.

#29 Tennessee Titans

Tennessee also finished 3-14, and their point differential was actually the single worst of any team in the league — minus 194. They didn't win a single divisional game all season, a first in franchise history. Brian Callahan got fired six games in. The one real positive was rookie quarterback Cam Ward, who set the franchise rookie passing record and looked much better down the stretch once he got some experience. That's actually the key to why Tennessee is ranked ahead of the three teams below them — the 2026 outlook is the best of this whole group. They hired Robert Saleh as head coach and, more importantly, Brian Daboll as offensive coordinator, who has an actual track record of developing young quarterbacks, most notably Josh Allen in Buffalo. Add a softer schedule and some positive regression coming after a bad run of one-score losses, and some models even have Tennessee sneaking into playoff contention.

#28 Cleveland Browns

Cleveland went 5-12, an improvement from the year before, but this was still one of the worst offenses in football — dead last in yards per play. What made it wild was the quarterback carousel — four guys competed for the job, Joe Flacco started and got traded away mid-season, and it eventually became rookie Shedeur Sanders' team by December. Meanwhile Myles Garrett set the all-time single-season sack record and won Defensive Player of the Year — and then Cleveland turned around and traded him to the Rams for a package built around future first-round picks. Kevin Stefanski was fired, and they hired Todd Monken, who developed Lamar Jackson in Baltimore, to try to fix the offense. There's real draft capital coming in 2027, but ownership has basically said this year is a bridge — they're not expecting to compete yet.
